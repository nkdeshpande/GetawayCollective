# ADR-0009 - Stage 1 precedes debt service

**Status:** Accepted, 31 Jul 2026
**Authority:** L1-16 §1.1a

## Context

The ratified waterfall puts the Operating Company ahead of both
constitutional reserves and debt service. Testing showed that a ninety
percent operating share on a one-million Revenue Base leaves the
administration reserve short, the sinking fund empty, and debt service
wholly unpaid.

## Decision

**Correct as ordered.** Debt is owned by the Investment Vehicle. The
Operating Company has nothing to do with it.

The operator's share is **consideration for services performed** - the same
economic class as the staff and utilities it pays out of that share - not a
claim on profit. The Vehicle services its own borrowing, and funds its own
reserve provisioning, out of what the property generates after it has paid
to be operated.

## Rejected

- Subordinating Stage 1 to Stages 3 through 5. The institutional norm, but
  it asks a service provider to underwrite a debt it neither incurred nor
  benefits from. No operator would accept the term.
- A constitutional cap on the Stage 1 share. The share is a commercial term
  that varies by asset; a fixed ceiling would go stale or force amendments.

## Consequences

- The starvation risk is real and **deliberately uncapped in the
  constitution**.
- The control is the Management Agreement, an always-material related-party
  transaction requiring Board approval to set and Board approval again to
  amend, regardless of value.
- The constitution governs who decides and on what evidence. The number
  belongs in the instrument the Board approves.
- The behaviour is pinned by a test so it cannot change silently.
