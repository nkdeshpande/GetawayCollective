# PART I — THE DISTRIBUTION WATERFALL

The waterfall is the spine of this document. Reserve funding, brand participation, and debt service are all defined by their position within it.

## 1.1 The Six-Stage Waterfall — RATIFIED

The constitutional distribution sequence for every LLP:

| Stage | Recipient | Basis | Governing Instrument |
|-------|-----------|-------|---------------------|
| **1** | **Operating Company** | Contractual revenue share + approved operating costs | Operating Agreement |
| **2** | **Brand & Digital Company** | Approved commercial participation | Commercial Services Agreement |
| **3** | **Enterprise Administration Reserve** | 2.5% of Revenue Base | This Constitution |
| **4** | **Property Sinking Fund** | 2.5% of Revenue Base | This Constitution |
| **5** | **Debt Service** | All scheduled principal, interest, financing obligations | Financing Documents |
| **6** | **LLP Partner Distributions** | Remaining distributable cash | LLP Agreement + Distribution Policy |

```
Revenue Base
     │
     ├─▶ [1] Operating Company        ── Operating Agreement
     │
     ├─▶ [2] Brand & Digital Company  ── Commercial Services Agreement
     │
     ├─▶ [3] Enterprise Admin Reserve ── 2.5%  (constitutional)
     │
     ├─▶ [4] Property Sinking Fund    ── 2.5%  (constitutional)
     │
     ├─▶ [5] Debt Service             ── financing documents
     │
     └─▶ [6] LLP PARTNER DISTRIBUTIONS
```

**No investor distribution shall occur while scheduled debt obligations remain unpaid (Stage 5 precedes Stage 6 absolutely).**

**Reserve transfers (Stages 3 and 4) occur before profit distributions to LLP Partners.**

> **SUPERSESSION NOTICE.** This waterfall replaces the earlier draft sequence *Revenue → Fees → Reserves → Preferred Return → Catch-Up → Profit Share* recorded in L1-01 §23. **There is no preferred return, no catch-up, and no carried interest tier.** Getaway Collective does not participate in LLP economics as an owner (see Part IV) and therefore has no promote to earn. Any document still referencing preferred return or catch-up is superseded.

## 1.2 The Revenue Base — RATIFIED

For all constitutional purposes, the **Revenue Base** means:

> The gross operating receipts of the LLP from property operations, **less only**, in respect of a booking:
> - statutory taxes collected on behalf of governmental authorities;
> - **booking platform fees** (the fee charged by the distribution platform through which the booking was made);
> - **channel / OTA commissions**;
> - **payment settlement and processing charges**; and
> - guest refunds and chargebacks.

> ⚠️ **"Platform fee" means the *booking platform's* fee — not Getaway Collective's Platform Administration Fee.**
>
> This distinction is constitutional, not clerical. GC's Platform Administration Fee is a Stage-1-adjacent service fee paid *out of* the waterfall. If it were instead deducted *before* the Revenue Base, GC's own compensation would rank ahead of the Enterprise Administration Reserve, the Property Sinking Fund, debt service, and every partner distribution — and it would shrink the base from which the Brand participation and both reserves are calculated. That is a fiduciary inversion and is prohibited.
>
> **No fee payable to Getaway Collective or to any affiliated division may be deducted in computing the Revenue Base.**

All other deductions — **operating costs, management fees, maintenance, reserves, financing costs, depreciation, and profit distributions** — are treated strictly in accordance with the approved waterfall and **shall not alter the Revenue Base** unless expressly authorised in the LLP Agreement.

**Why this definition is constitutional rather than accounting-derived.** "Net Revenue," "Net Operating Revenue," and "NOI" are each interpreted differently across operators, auditors, and jurisdictions. Because Stages 2, 3, and 4 are all expressed as percentages of this figure, an ambiguous base would make three constitutional obligations unenforceable simultaneously. The Revenue Base is defined once, here, and applied identically across every LLP.

# PART II — RESERVE POLICY (BLANK-09 RATIFIED)

## 2.1 Constitutional Principle

Every LLP shall maintain a mandatory **Reserve Fund** to ensure the continued operation, maintenance, and preservation of the Property **without requiring investor capital calls for normal operations**.

Once a Property has achieved **Operational Stabilisation**, the Reserve Fund shall be maintained through recurring allocations from operating revenue rather than additional investor capital.

**The Reserve Fund shall never be used for ordinary operating expenses that are contractually the responsibility of the Operating Company.**

## 2.2 Two Reserves, Two Purposes

| Reserve | Allocation | Funds |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| **Enterprise Administration Reserve** | 2.5% of Revenue Base | Governance · statutory compliance · audits · insurance · investor reporting · legal administration · enterprise oversight |
| **Property Sinking Fund** | 2.5% of Revenue Base | *Exclusively* long-term capital preservation and replacement of major property assets: building fabric, MEP systems, infrastructure, furniture, fixtures, equipment, and other lifecycle renewals |

Both transfers occur at Stages 3 and 4 of the waterfall — **before profit distributions to LLP Partners**.

## 2.3 The Reserve Floor

The minimum Reserve Floor shall equal **the greater of**:

1. **Six (6) months of the LLP's non-operational fixed obligations** — governance, statutory, insurance, financing, and enterprise administration costs; **or**
2. **The Board-approved minimum reserve** determined through the Annual Asset Management Plan.

**Explicitly excluded from the Reserve Floor:** the Operating Company's day-to-day operating expenses. Those are funded and managed through the approved Operating Budget under the Operating Agreement, and do not form part of this calculation.

> **Note on what this floor is not.** The floor is deliberately **not** expressed as a percentage of NAV. NAV is a valuation metric; liquidity risk is a cash-flow problem. Institutional real estate platforms separate Liquidity Reserves from Asset Value, and this Constitution does the same.

## 2.4 Scope — Per-LLP, Not Pooled

- The Reserve Floor is maintained at the **LLP (Property SPV) level**.
- **Each LLP maintains its own independent reserve.**
- Enterprise-level reserves maintained by Getaway Collective **supplement but never replace** the mandatory reserve obligations of an individual LLP.
- **Reserve balances shall not be pooled between LLPs** except where expressly approved by the Board under an Enterprise Treasury Policy.

## 2.5 Alert Thresholds

| Reserve Level | Status | Required Action |
|---------------|--------|-----------------|
| **≥ 120%** of Reserve Floor | Healthy | Normal operations |
| **110 – 119%** | Advisory | Management monitoring |
| **100 – 109%** | Governance Alert | Executive review and corrective planning |
| **< 100%** | **Constitutional Breach** | Distribution freeze and mandatory Board action |

## 2.6 Breach Response — AMENDED 30 Jul 2026

Where the Reserve Balance falls below the Reserve Floor, the response is deliberately **minimal and automatic**:

1. **Broadcast.** A reserve breach notification is issued to the Board, the Executive Office, and affected LLP Partners.
2. **Expenditure deferral.** Discretionary expenditure is deferred until the reserve is restored, provided the deferral does not compromise asset integrity.
3. **All discretionary distributions to LLP Partners are suspended** until the reserve is restored.

> **A reserve breach shall never trigger a capital call.**
>
> This follows directly from §2.7. Investor capital is growth capital. A reserve shortfall is an operating and expenditure problem, and it is solved by deferring spend and pausing distributions — never by asking investors for money to cover it.

**Permitted corrective actions:** expenditure deferral · adjustment of reserve allocations · suspension of distributions · refinancing or treasury measures · other Board-approved recovery actions **that do not draw on investor capital**.

> **AMENDMENT NOTICE.** An earlier draft of this section prescribed a 5-business-day Board notification, a 15-business-day Investment Committee Reserve Recovery Plan, and a 30-calendar-day Board determination. That escalation machinery is **repealed** in favour of the automatic response above. Rationale: a reserve breach is a known condition with a known remedy; it does not require a bespoke Board process each time it occurs. The Board is informed by broadcast and retains authority to intervene, but no intervention is *required* for the corrective mechanism to operate.

> ⚠️ **Open interpretation — flagged for confirmation.** The instruction was *"just a broadcast is sent and expenses are delayed."* Item 3 (distribution suspension) has been **retained**, because your BLANK-09 ruling stated it explicitly and the later instruction did not say to remove it. If distributions should in fact continue during a reserve breach, this is the line to strike — but note that doing so would allow partner distributions to continue while the reserve is below floor, which materially weakens invariant **F-04**.

## 2.7 Capital Calls — Growth Capital, Not Working Capital

Following Operational Stabilisation, **capital calls shall not be used to fund**:
- ordinary operating deficits
- routine maintenance
- reserve replenishment

**Capital calls are permitted only for:**
- acquisition of new assets
- Board-approved expansion or redevelopment
- extraordinary events outside the normal operating and reserve framework
- circumstances requiring approval under the LLP Agreement by the requisite partner vote

> **This establishes the constitutional principle that investor capital is growth capital, not working capital.**

## 2.8 Asset Lifecycle Application

| Phase | Reserve Obligation |
|-------|--------------------|
| **Development** | Reserve requirements funded from the approved project capital budget |
| **Stabilisation** | Upon achieving Operational Stabilisation, the recurring 2.5% + 2.5% funding mechanism becomes **mandatory** |
| **Mature Asset** | Reserve Floor maintained continuously; distributions permitted only after all reserve obligations satisfied |

# PART III — LEVERAGE & BORROWING (BLANK-21 RATIFIED)

## 3.1 Constitutional Position: No Hard-Coded Ratios

**No enterprise-wide LTV, portfolio leverage ratio, or DSCR threshold is constitutionally prescribed.**

**Reasoning.** Unlike a REIT or closed-end fund, this platform establishes multiple LLPs with materially different asset classes, acquisition strategies, lender requirements, capital structures, development profiles, and risk tolerances. A constitutional LTV of "60%" would either become obsolete or force unnecessary constitutional amendments.

**The Constitution therefore governs the approval process, authority, disclosure, and investor protection. The numbers are set per-LLP.**

## 3.2 Per-LLP Determination

Maximum permitted leverage for each Property LLP is determined individually by the Investment Committee as part of the financing strategy, considering: property type · acquisition structure · development profile · projected cash flows · lender requirements · market conditions · investor risk profile · long-term financial sustainability.

**The approved leverage limit shall be documented in the Investment Memorandum and Financing Plan for the relevant LLP before debt is incurred.**

## 3.3 Nature of the Limit — Hard Once Approved

The borrowing limit approved for each LLP is a **hard limit for that LLP**.

Borrowings exceeding the Board-approved financing limits require:
1. a revised financing proposal;
2. renewed Investment Committee recommendation; **and**
3. approval by **Special Resolution (≥76%)** per EP-01 §3.8 where the revised borrowing materially alters the investment risk accepted by LLP Partners.

> *This is what makes the EP-01 §3.8 Special Resolution trigger operable.* Previously the trigger referenced "approved leverage limits" that did not exist, making it structurally unreachable. Each LLP's approved financing limit is now the reference point.

## 3.4 Asset-Stage Variation

Different leverage profiles may be approved for different lifecycle stages: acquisition · development/construction · stabilisation · operational maturity · refurbishment/redevelopment.

Applicable parameters for each stage shall be disclosed in the approved Financing Strategy. **Any material deviation requires the same governance approvals as an amendment to the financing strategy.**

## 3.5 Debt Service Coverage

Minimum DSCR requirements are determined during underwriting · through lender covenants · by the Investment Committee · within the financing documents applicable to the relevant LLP.

The Executive Office shall monitor compliance with all financing covenants and **promptly report any actual or anticipated breach** to the Board and the Audit & Risk Committee.

## 3.6 Recourse vs Non-Recourse

Neither is mandated. Structure determined transaction-by-transaction following assessment of commercial terms · cost of capital · lender requirements · investor protections · enterprise risk · strategic objectives.

**The nature of the financing shall be fully disclosed to LLP Partners before commitment of capital.**

## 3.7 Constitutional Borrowing Principles

Regardless of structure, every borrowing shall satisfy:

1. Borrowing shall support the approved investment strategy of the LLP.
2. Debt shall not expose LLP Partners to undisclosed or disproportionate financial risk.
3. All material financing terms shall be transparently disclosed before investor commitment.
4. The Investment Committee shall certify that the proposed financing is prudent and appropriate for the asset and its lifecycle.
5. Any borrowing materially changing the approved financial risk profile requires enhanced governance approval per EP-01 §3.8.

# PART IV — BRAND & DIGITAL PARTICIPATION (BLANK-22 RATIFIED)

## 4.1 Constitutional Principle

The Brand & Digital Company is appointed by the LLP to provide branding, digital platforms, marketing, customer acquisition, revenue optimisation, and commercial growth services.

**Its participation is consideration for contracted services. It does not constitute an ownership interest in the LLP or its assets.**

Accordingly:
- Brand participation shall **never dilute** LLP ownership interests.
- Brand participation shall **never confer** governance or voting rights.
- Brand participation shall be governed **exclusively** through a Board-approved Commercial Services Agreement.
- Brand participation shall be **fully disclosed** to LLP Partners before capital commitment.

## 4.2 Participation Basis — Property-Specific

The Brand & Digital Company receives a contractually agreed percentage of the Revenue Base, as specified in the Commercial Services Agreement approved for the relevant LLP.

The model is **LLP-specific**, determined during underwriting based on: market positioning · commercial strategy · expected marketing intensity · digital platform requirements · projected operating economics · investor return objectives.

**The Constitution intentionally does not prescribe a fixed percentage.**

## 4.3 Rate Approval Chain

The applicable participation percentage shall be:
1. approved by the Investment Committee;
2. ratified by the Board;
3. disclosed in the Investment Memorandum;
4. incorporated into the LLP financial model **before investor subscription**; and
5. fixed for the term of the agreement unless amended through the governance process.

## 4.4 Position in the Waterfall

**Stage 2** — after the Operating Company (Stage 1), before Enterprise Administration Reserve (Stage 3).

> **On the seniority question.** Brand participation is calculated from the Revenue Base and paid at Stage 2, which places it ahead of reserves, debt service, and partner distributions. This does **not** make it senior to investor economics in the objectionable sense, because it is an **approved operating allocation within the LLP's annual operating budget** — consideration for services rendered — not a claim on profit. The constitutional protections in §4.1 (no dilution, no governance rights, no ownership) are what preserve investor primacy, not waterfall position.

## 4.5 Performance Conditions

The Commercial Services Agreement shall define measurable KPIs, which may include: occupancy · direct booking mix · customer acquisition efficiency · revenue growth · brand strength · digital conversion · customer satisfaction · other metrics appropriate to the LLP.

Where performance standards are **materially and persistently not achieved**, the Board may require:
- corrective action plans
- fee adjustments
- performance-based reductions
- replacement of the Brand & Digital Company
- termination per the Commercial Services Agreement

## 4.6 Review and Reset

Reviewed: annually as part of the LLP budgeting process · upon renewal or amendment of the Commercial Services Agreement · following any material change in commercial strategy · where directed by the Board.

**Any amendment affecting the approved participation percentage requires the same governance approvals as the original agreement.**

## 4.7 Fairness Benchmark

Before approval or renewal, the Board shall be satisfied the participation is **commercially reasonable**, assessing:
- comparable hospitality marketing and distribution agreements
- prevailing asset management and commercial services fees for similar assets
- scope and quality of services provided
- historical performance of the Brand & Digital Company
- **impact on LLP Partner returns**

The basis for the Board's determination shall be documented in the transaction records and retained as part of the governance record.
