# 1. PURPOSE

The Enterprise Policy Framework establishes the mandatory principles, governance controls, decision rights, operational boundaries, and compliance obligations governing Getaway Collective as a private investment platform for experiential real estate.

These are **not** HR or corporate-miscellany policies. They function as the **operating law of the enterprise** — binding rules governing capital, assets, governance, stewardship, technology, and fiduciary responsibility. Each is an auditable control document.

Policies are mandatory and supersede operating procedures, work instructions, and internal guidelines where conflicts exist.

**Scope of application:**
Enterprise Governance · Investment Management · Portfolio Management · Capital Formation · Property Stewardship · Technology Platforms · Data Governance · Financial Management · Risk Management · Third-Party Relationships

## §1 — Purpose, Objectives, Scope & Principles

### 1.1 Purpose

The Enterprise Governance Policy establishes the governance framework through which Getaway Collective exercises fiduciary responsibility, protects investor interests, preserves institutional integrity, and ensures disciplined stewardship of capital, assets, and enterprise resources.

It defines the principles by which authority is exercised, decisions are made, responsibilities are allocated, and accountability is maintained across every legal entity, investment vehicle, and operating relationship.

Governance exists to ensure that every decision advances the long-term interests of investors while preserving the constitutional values of transparency, prudence, stewardship, and institutional trust.

This policy is the highest operational governance authority beneath the Enterprise Constitution and governs all enterprise decision-making unless superseded by applicable law or constitutional amendment.

### 1.2 Objectives

- Establish a clear governance structure for the enterprise
- Define decision-making authority across all levels of governance
- Preserve fiduciary accountability to investors
- Ensure disciplined allocation and protection of capital
- Maintain transparent governance processes
- Prevent concentration of unchecked authority
- Ensure consistent application of enterprise policies
- Provide confidence to investors, regulators, partners, and stakeholders
- Safeguard the long-term sustainability of the enterprise

### 1.3 Scope

Applies to every governance activity undertaken by Getaway Collective:

| Domain | Covers |
|--------|--------|
| **Enterprise Governance** | Corporate governance · enterprise oversight · constitutional governance · governance reporting · strategic decision-making |
| **Investment Governance** | Investment Committee decisions · capital allocation · investment approvals · portfolio strategy · acquisition approvals · disposition approvals |
| **Financial Governance** | Treasury oversight · reserve management · financial approvals · distribution approvals · budget governance |
| **Portfolio Governance** | Portfolio construction · asset concentration · diversification · geographic allocation · performance review |
| **Risk Governance** | Enterprise risk oversight · investment risk · compliance oversight · ESG oversight · enterprise-level operational risk · technology governance |
| **Legal Governance** | Corporate structure · investment vehicles · SPVs · trusts · regulatory compliance · legal agreements |
| **Third-Party Governance** | Operating Partner appointments · investment advisors · auditors · valuation firms · legal advisors · external service providers |

### 1.4 Applicability

Mandatory for: Board of Directors · Executive Office · Investment Committee · Audit & Risk Committee · Governance Committee · Portfolio Committee · Senior Leadership · Employees · Advisors · Contractors · Approved Representatives · External Parties acting under delegated authority.

**No person acting on behalf of Getaway Collective is exempt from this policy unless explicitly required by applicable law.**

### 1.5 Governance Philosophy

Getaway Collective operates as an institutional investment platform founded upon the principle that **governance exists to protect capital before it enables growth**.

Governance is not an administrative function. It is the primary mechanism through which investor trust is preserved.

Every governance decision shall balance five constitutional objectives:

1. Preservation of investor capital
2. Responsible long-term growth
3. Prudent management of enterprise risk
4. Transparent and accountable decision-making
5. Stewardship of exceptional real estate assets

**Short-term commercial opportunity shall never override these objectives.**

### 1.7 Governance Principles (GP-01 … GP-10)

## §2 — Governance Architecture, Authority Structure & Decision Rights

### 2.1 Governance Architecture

Getaway Collective operates through a **multi-entity enterprise architecture** separating capital ownership, operational execution, and commercial brand activities. The enterprise adopts a constitutional principle of **functional independence**: each entity performs a distinct role while remaining contractually accountable to the investment platform.

> **No entity shall simultaneously control investment governance, operational execution, and commercial demand generation.**

This structural separation preserves fiduciary independence and enables objective performance evaluation.

### 2.2 Constitutional Enterprise Roles

#### A. Getaway Collective — Investment Platform and Asset Steward

Getaway Collective exists to: acquire assets · structure investment vehicles · raise investor capital · own property · preserve capital · appoint operating partners · appoint commercial partners · govern enterprise performance · distribute economic returns.

**Getaway Collective does not directly provide hospitality services. It governs the organisations that do.**

Exclusive authority over: Investment Strategy · Capital Formation · Property Acquisition · Property Disposal · Portfolio Construction · Capital Allocation · Investor Relations · Governance · Financial Reporting · Valuation · Risk Management · Enterprise Policies · Operating Partner Appointment · Brand Partner Appointment · Distribution Policy · Enterprise Compliance · Long-Term Asset Stewardship.

#### B. Operating Company — Independent Property Operating Partner

Responsible for transforming investment assets into operational businesses capable of producing sustainable income. Acts under a formal Management Agreement approved by Getaway Collective.

**Responsibilities:** Property Operations · Hospitality Operations · Revenue Operations · Staff Management · Facilities Management · Maintenance · Guest Services · Procurement · Vendor Management · Safety · Operational Compliance · Budget Execution.

**The Operating Company shall not:** issue investment products · make capital allocation decisions · alter ownership structures · amend governance · appoint directors · represent investors.

#### C. Brand & Digital Company — Independent Commercial Growth Partner

Exists to maximise long-term commercial performance of the investment portfolio through brand development, digital products, marketing, customer acquisition, and demand generation.

*It is not a property owner. It is not an investment manager. It is not a hospitality operator. It is a commercial platform.*

**Responsibilities:** Brand Strategy · Digital Products · Web Platforms · CRM · Performance Marketing · Content · Sales Enablement · Community · Market Intelligence · Demand Forecasting · Distribution Strategy · Partnership Marketing · Customer Acquisition · Digital Analytics.

The Brand Company may receive a constitutionally approved commercial participation based upon Net Operating Revenue generated by assets under management. Such participation shall: be contractually defined · remain transparent · be independently auditable · **never dilute investor ownership** · **never subordinate investor distributions**.

## §3 — Board Governance, Committees, Delegations & Voting Framework

### 3.1 Vertical Integration Philosophy

Getaway Collective is a **vertically integrated investment enterprise** whose purpose is the acquisition, ownership, governance, development, operation, branding, and long-term stewardship of experiential real estate assets.

The enterprise may own and operate multiple divisions under a common corporate structure: Investment Management · Property Operations · Brand & Digital · Technology Platforms · Shared Services.

**Notwithstanding this vertical integration, fiduciary governance shall remain independent from operational and commercial management.** The Board shall always govern in the interests of investors before the interests of any individual operating division.

### 3.2 Constitutional Enterprise Structure

```
                    GETAWAY COLLECTIVE LLP
               Enterprise Holding & Governance
                           │
    ┌──────────────┬───────────────┬───────────────┐
    │              │               │
    ▼              ▼               ▼
Investment    Operating Division   Brand & Digital
Division      (Sensory Getaways)      Division
    │              │               │
    └──────────────┴───────────────┘
                Shared Services
```

The enterprise operates as **one legal group** while maintaining governance separation between fiduciary decisions and operational execution.

### 3.3 Legal Structure

Until otherwise approved by Constitutional Amendment, **every investment vehicle established by Getaway Collective shall be constituted as a Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)** under applicable law.

No alternative investment vehicle (private limited companies, trusts, REITs, corporate partnerships) may be established without Board approval and constitutional review.

Each LLP is an independent legal entity maintaining independent: accounts · statutory filings · partner registers · financial statements · governance records. The enterprise may simultaneously manage multiple LLPs.

### 3.4 Board of Directors / Designated Partners

The Board (or Designated Partners under LLP legislation) is the highest governing authority. The Board acts as **trustee of investor interests and guardian of the Enterprise Constitution**. The Board shall not participate in operational management except where expressly required by law or constitutional authority.

**Responsibilities:** enterprise strategy · constitutional stewardship · appointment of Executive Leadership · appointment of committee members · approval of enterprise policies · approval of investment strategy · approval of annual budgets · approval of acquisitions and disposals · approval of capital raising · approval of distributions · enterprise risk oversight · succession planning.

The Board remains collectively accountable for fiduciary stewardship.

### 3.5 Governance Committees

Committees provide recommendations. Committees do not replace Board authority unless expressly delegated.

| Committee | Purpose | Responsibilities |
|-----------|---------|------------------|
| **A. Investment Committee** | Approve capital deployment | Acquisitions · dispositions · underwriting · valuation review · capital allocation · financing · refinancing · investment memoranda · investment performance. *Authority: recommend or approve within delegated limits.* |
| **B. Audit & Risk Committee** | Protect enterprise integrity | Financial reporting · internal audit · reserves · treasury · enterprise risk · cyber risk · fraud · compliance · insurance · external audit |
| **C. Governance & Ethics Committee** | Protect governance quality | Constitutional interpretation · conflicts of interest · director independence · governance reviews · policy oversight · whistleblower matters · ethics investigations |
| **D. Brand & Market Committee** | Protect brand while supporting growth | Brand strategy · positioning · marketing effectiveness · customer acquisition · digital platforms · reputation · communications. **Shall not approve capital allocation or investment decisions.** |
| **E. Operations & Asset Performance Committee** | Monitor operational performance of assets | Operating company performance · operating agreements · service levels · property performance · operational budgets · maintenance strategy · ESG performance. **Oversees operations; does not manage day-to-day hospitality.** |

### 3.6 Delegation of Authority

| Function | Board | Executive Office | Division Head |
|----------|-------|------------------|---------------|
| Constitution | Final Authority | Recommend | None |
| Investment Strategy | Final Authority | Execute | None |
| Property Acquisition | Approve | Recommend | Due Diligence Only |
| Portfolio Strategy | Approve | Execute | None |
| Hospitality Operations | Oversight | Delegate | Execute |
| Brand Strategy | Oversight | Delegate | Execute |
| Technology | Oversight | Execute | Execute |
| Finance | Oversight | Execute | Budget Authority |
| HR | Policy | Execute | Manage Teams |

Delegated authority shall always be documented. **Delegation never transfers fiduciary accountability.**

### 3.7 Conflict of Interest Framework

Because Getaway Collective owns both the Operating Division and the Brand & Digital Division, potential conflicts are **recognised as inherent to the enterprise structure**.

- **Investment Decisions.** Made solely in the interests of investors. Commercial interests of operating divisions shall not influence investment approvals.
- **Operating Agreements.** Every Operating Division shall operate under a written Operating Agreement approved by the Board, defining responsibilities, service levels, reporting, performance metrics, termination rights, financial arrangements. *The Operating Division shall be evaluated as if it were an independent third-party operator.*
- **Brand & Digital Agreements.** Operate under a written Commercial Services Agreement. Revenue participation shall be transparent · independently documented · Board-approved · benchmarked for fairness · periodically reviewed. **No marketing fee or revenue share may be amended without Board approval.**
- **Related-Party Transactions.** Transactions between Investment / Operating / Brand / Technology Divisions shall be documented · disclose commercial terms · be benchmarked where practical · receive independent review where material · be reported to the Audit & Risk Committee.

### 3.8 Voting Framework — RATIFIED

| Resolution | Threshold | Applies To |
|------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Ordinary** | **> 50%** of voting rights present | Annual budgets · committee appointments · operating policies · routine governance matters |
| **Special** | **≥ 76%** of voting rights | Constitutional amendments · acquisition or disposal of material assets · admission of new partners · removal of designated partners · changes to ownership structure · borrowing beyond approved leverage limits · merger, dissolution, or restructuring of an LLP · related-party transactions above Board-defined materiality threshold · amendments to management, operating, or brand participation agreements materially affecting investor economics |
| **Quorum** | **≥ 60%** of total voting rights present in person or by valid proxy | All meetings. Failure requires adjournment and reconvening per the LLP Agreement. |

**Proxy Voting.** Permitted. Shall be in writing · relate to one meeting only unless otherwise specified · may not be re-delegated · shall be retained with meeting records.

**Tie Resolution.** A tied resolution is deemed **NOT APPROVED**. The burden of approval rests with the proposer. No casting vote shall exist unless expressly provided in the LLP Agreement for administrative matters that do not affect investor rights, capital allocation, or governance.

*This section resolves BLANK-08 and is the sole source for `voting.ts`.*

### 3.9 Governance Independence

Although Getaway Collective operates integrated investment, operating, and commercial businesses, **the Investment Division shall remain constitutionally superior in matters affecting investor capital, ownership, governance, and fiduciary responsibility.**

Neither the Operating Division nor the Brand & Digital Division may override, delay, or influence decisions relating to: capital formation · investor rights · property acquisition or disposal · portfolio construction · distributions · constitutional governance.

## §4 — Fiduciary Duties, Conflicts of Interest, Ethics, Transparency & Accountability

### 4.1 Purpose

Establishes the fiduciary obligations, ethical standards, conflict management principles, transparency requirements, and accountability framework governing Getaway Collective LLP.

Every Director, Designated Partner, Committee Member, Executive, Employee, Advisor, and Authorised Representative shall act as a **fiduciary steward of investor capital and enterprise assets**. These obligations prevail over commercial convenience, operational expediency, departmental interests, or personal benefit.

### 4.2 Constitutional Principle of Fiduciary Primacy

The primary fiduciary obligation is to preserve and enhance investor capital through prudent stewardship of assets, disciplined governance, and responsible long-term decision-making.

**The Four Constitutional Questions.** Every governance decision shall be capable of answering:

1. Does this decision protect investor interests?
2. Does this decision improve long-term enterprise value?
3. Does this decision preserve the integrity of the investment platform?
4. Could this decision withstand independent external scrutiny?

**If any answer cannot be reasonably demonstrated, the decision shall not proceed without further review.**

### 4.3 Fiduciary Duties (FD-01 … FD-06)

| ID | Duty | Obligation |
|----|------|------------|
| **FD-01** | **Loyalty** | All decisions made solely in the best interests of the investment vehicle and its investors. Personal interests, departmental objectives, commercial relationships, or related-party benefits never take precedence. |
| **FD-02** | **Care** | Reasonable diligence, professional judgement, and informed analysis before any material decision. Supported by verified information · financial analysis · documented risk assessment · legal review where applicable · recorded rationale. |
| **FD-03** | **Prudence** | Capital deployed conservatively and responsibly. Speculative behaviour inconsistent with approved investment strategy is prohibited. Every investment shall demonstrate acceptable balance between risk and expected long-term return. |
| **FD-04** | **Stewardship** | Every property managed as a long-term institutional asset. Asset quality shall not be compromised to improve short-term financial performance. Deferred expenditure that materially diminishes long-term value shall be avoided unless specifically Board-approved. |
| **FD-05** | **Confidentiality** | Confidential information used solely for legitimate enterprise purposes. Information concerning investors, acquisitions, valuations, negotiations, financial performance, or strategic initiatives shall not be disclosed except through authorised channels. |
| **FD-06** | **Independence** | Governance participants shall remain free from undue influence. Professional judgement shall not be impaired by financial incentives, employment relationships, family interests, political considerations, or commercial pressure. |

### 4.4 Fiduciary Responsibilities of the Board

The Board acts as **trustee of the enterprise**, not representative of any individual investor, division, or commercial interest.

The Board shall: preserve enterprise integrity · safeguard investor capital · oversee enterprise risk · ensure constitutional compliance · appoint competent executive leadership · maintain institutional independence · oversee related-party transactions · protect minority investor interests · ensure equitable treatment of all LLP partners.

**The Board shall not use its authority to favour any internal business division or affiliated entity.**

### 4.5 Fiduciary Responsibilities of Executive Management

Executives shall: execute Board decisions faithfully · maintain accurate reporting · preserve enterprise assets · disclose material risks promptly · prevent conflicts of interest · supervise delegated authority · promote ethical conduct throughout the enterprise.

Executive performance shall be evaluated against **long-term enterprise outcomes**, not short-term financial performance alone.

### 4.6 Conflicts of Interest — Constitutional Principle

Because the enterprise comprises Investment Management, Operating Division, Brand & Digital Division, Technology Platforms, and Shared Services, conflicts of interest are recognised as an **inherent governance risk**.

> **Conflicts are not prohibited. Undisclosed or unmanaged conflicts are prohibited.**

### 4.7 Types of Conflicts

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Personal** | A governance participant may obtain personal financial benefit from a decision |
| **Financial** | An individual or related party has an ownership interest in a transaction being considered |
| **Employment** | A decision materially benefits an employer, subsidiary, affiliate, or business unit in which the decision-maker has executive responsibility |
| **Related-Party** | Transactions between GC LLP · Operating Division · Brand & Digital Division · Technology Division · Directors · Designated Partners · family members · controlled entities |
| **Competitive** | A governance participant possesses interests that compete with the enterprise |
| **Information** | Access to confidential enterprise information may create unfair commercial advantage |

### 4.8 Conflict Disclosure

Every Director, Partner, Executive, Committee Member, and Advisor shall disclose any actual, perceived, or potential conflict **immediately upon becoming aware of its existence**.

Disclosure shall occur **before** discussion · **before** deliberation · **before** voting · **before** execution.

**Failure to disclose constitutes a governance breach.** → Enforced by invariant **I-07 (Conflicts Are Disclosed)**.

### 4.9 Conflict Register

The Office of Enterprise Governance shall maintain a permanent Conflict Register recording: individual · nature of conflict · affected matter · date disclosed · mitigation measures · recusal requirements · Board determination · resolution status.

Reviewed annually by the Governance & Ethics Committee.

### 4.10 Related-Party Transactions

All inter-division commercial arrangements are treated as Related-Party Transactions: Management Agreements · Brand Licensing · Revenue Participation Agreements · Technology Services · Shared Services · Marketing Services · Development Management.

Such transactions shall: be documented through written agreements · be commercially reasonable · be benchmarked where practical · disclose pricing methodology · disclose revenue sharing methodology · receive Board approval where material.

**No internal division shall receive preferential commercial treatment that materially disadvantages investors.**

### 4.10a Materiality Threshold — RATIFIED 30 Jul 2026

*Resolves BLANK-13b. This threshold is what makes §3.8 and §4.10 operable — both previously referenced "material" without defining it.*

A related-party transaction is **material** where it meets **any one** of:

| Test | Trigger |
|------|---------|
| **Value** | Annual value exceeds **₹50 lakh** |
| **Proportion** | Annual value exceeds **2%** of the relevant SPV's annual operating expense |
| **Duration** | Term exceeds **36 months** |
| **Lock-in** | Creates an exclusivity, non-compete, or termination-restriction affecting the SPV |

**Consequences by band:**

| Band | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| **Below threshold** | Documented · benchmarked where practical · reported **quarterly** to Audit & Risk |
| **At or above threshold** | **Board approval** required |
| **Above ₹5 crore** | Board approval **plus Special Resolution (≥76%)** per §3.8 |

**Always material regardless of value:**
- the **Management Agreement** (Operating Company)
- the **Brand Participation Agreement** / Commercial Services Agreement (Brand & Digital Company)
- **any amendment to either**

> **Why the always-material carve-out exists.** These two agreements define the economics of Stages 1 and 2 of the distribution waterfall (L1-16 Part I). A nominally small amendment to either — a fractional rate change, a redefinition of a deduction — compounds across every booking for the life of the asset. Annual value is the wrong measure for an instrument whose effect is structural.

**Note on scope.** Because Getaway Collective holds no equity in the LLPs it governs (L1-01 §27), *every* economic relationship between GC and an LLP runs through a service agreement, and every one is a related-party transaction. This threshold is therefore load-bearing across the entire GC fee model, not an edge-case control.

### 4.11 Management of Internal Divisions

Each division shall be managed **as though it were an independent commercial service provider**:

- performance measured independently
- service agreements define measurable obligations
- financial performance separately reported
- service fees transparent
- Board oversight evaluates commercial effectiveness **without assuming automatic continuation**

### 4.12 Ethics

Zero tolerance for: fraud · corruption · bribery · coercion · market manipulation · financial misrepresentation · deliberate concealment · misuse of investor funds · retaliation against whistleblowers.

**Ethical behaviour shall not be compromised for commercial advantage.**

### 4.13 Gifts, Hospitality & Benefits

No individual may accept gifts, hospitality, entertainment, commissions, discounts, or personal benefits capable of influencing objective judgement.

Nominal business courtesies consistent with customary professional practice may be accepted where disclosure is made · value remains insignificant · independence is not compromised. Material benefits require disclosure and approval.

### 4.14 Enterprise Transparency

Transparency is a constitutional obligation. Complete and accurate records shall be maintained concerning: governance decisions · investor communications · financial performance · valuations · distributions · related-party transactions · committee decisions · material risks.

**Transparency shall extend equally to favourable and unfavourable information.**

### 4.15 Accountability

Every governance decision shall identify: Decision Authority · Responsible Executive · Approving Body · Supporting Evidence · Decision Date · Expected Outcome · Review Date.

> **Authority may be delegated. Accountability may not.**
> **No decision shall exist without an accountable owner.**

→ Enforced by invariant **E-02 (Every Decision Has Provenance)**.

### 4.16 Mandatory Enterprise Records

Board Minutes · Committee Minutes · Conflict Register · Related-Party Register · Governance Register · Decision Register · Delegation Register · Policy Register · Ethics Declarations · Annual Conflict Declarations.

These constitute the official governance history of the enterprise.

### 4.17 Whistleblower Protection

Any individual may report suspected misconduct, unethical behaviour, financial irregularity, governance failure, or breach of fiduciary duty **without fear of retaliation**. Reports shall be investigated independently. Retaliation constitutes a serious governance violation and may result in removal from office or termination of engagement.

### 4.18 Accountability to Investors

The Board and Executive Office are accountable to the LLP Partners collectively, demonstrated through: accurate financial reporting · timely governance reporting · transparent disclosure of material events · prudent stewardship of assets · disciplined capital allocation · independent oversight of internal operating and brand divisions · consistent adherence to the Enterprise Constitution and Policy Framework.

**The confidence of investors is recognised as the enterprise's most valuable asset.**

## §5 — Records, Compliance, Exceptions, Policy Administration & Review

### 5.1 Purpose

Establishes the mandatory governance records, compliance obligations, policy administration framework, review cycles, accountability assignments, and evidence requirements.

> Every governance decision shall produce evidence.
> Every governance activity shall have an accountable owner.
> Every governance record shall have a defined custodian.
> **Governance is incomplete until its supporting records have been formally captured and preserved.**

### 5.2 Governance Record Principles

| Principle | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Completeness** | All material governance activities fully documented |
| **Authenticity** | Every record identifies Author · Approver · Date · Version · Source · Status |
| **Traceability** | Constitution → Enterprise Policy → Board Resolution → Committee Review → Executive Approval → Implementation → Evidence → Audit |
| **Integrity** | Records never altered without version history. Superseded records permanently archived. |
| **Accessibility** | Authorised participants have timely access appropriate to their role |

### 5.3 Mandatory Governance Registers

| Register | Purpose | Record Owner |
|----------|---------|--------------|
| Enterprise Governance Register | Master governance inventory | Governance Office |
| Board Resolution Register | All Board decisions | Board Secretary |
| Committee Register | Committee composition and authority | Governance Office |
| Delegation Register | Delegated authorities | Executive Office |
| Policy Register | Enterprise policy library | Governance Office |
| Conflict of Interest Register | Declared conflicts | Governance Office |
| Related Party Register | Related-party transactions | Audit & Risk |
| Investment Approval Register | Approved investments | Investment Office |
| Decision Register | Material enterprise decisions | Governance Office |
| Risk Register | Enterprise risks | Risk Office |
| Compliance Register | Regulatory obligations | Compliance Office |
| Litigation Register | Legal matters | General Counsel |
| Investor Communications Register | Formal investor notices | Investor Relations |
| Constitutional Amendment Register | Constitutional changes | Governance Office |

### 5.4 Records Retention

All governance records retained for the life of the relevant LLP plus the applicable statutory retention period, whichever is longer.

**Permanently retained (never destroyed except where required by law):** Enterprise Constitution · LLP Agreement · Constitutional Amendments · Board Minutes · Committee Minutes · Investor Register · Capital Register · Ownership Register · Annual Financial Statements · Independent Valuations · External Audit Reports · Material Legal Agreements.

### 5.5 Three-Layer Compliance Framework

| Layer | Ensures Adherence To | Owner |
|-------|---------------------|-------|
| **Layer 1 — Constitutional** | Enterprise Constitution · Enterprise Policies · LLP Agreement | Enterprise Governance Office |
| **Layer 2 — Regulatory** | LLP legislation · corporate law · tax law · AML/KYC · financial reporting · data protection · labour legislation · environmental regulations | Compliance Office |
| **Layer 3 — Commercial** | Operating Agreements · Brand Participation Agreements · Investment Agreements · Loan Covenants · Vendor Agreements · Insurance Requirements | Executive Office |

### 5.6 Compliance Reporting

Reports shall cover: policy compliance · governance exceptions · outstanding actions · regulatory changes · audit findings · risk exposures · related-party transactions · conflict disclosures · material breaches · corrective actions.

Reports shall classify each item as: **Compliant · Non-Compliant · Partially Compliant · Not Applicable**.

### 5.7 Governance Exceptions

Exceptions shall be **rare · documented · risk assessed · time limited · formally approved**.

**No exception may permanently override an Enterprise Policy without Constitutional Amendment.**

| Category | Description | Approval |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **Administrative** | Low-risk procedural deviations | Executive Office |
| **Commercial** | Temporary commercial deviations | Relevant Committee |
| **Governance** | Deviation affecting governance | Board |
| **Constitutional** | Deviation affecting constitutional principles | **NOT PERMITTED** — requires Constitutional Amendment |

### 5.8 Exception Register

Every approved exception recorded with: Exception ID · policy affected · reason · business justification · risk assessment · approving authority · duration · review date · closure date · residual risk.

### 5.9 Policy Administration

Administered centrally by the Office of Enterprise Governance: maintain policy versions · manage approvals · coordinate reviews · publish updates · maintain historical versions · track policy acknowledgements · maintain policy dependencies · coordinate constitutional alignment.

**No enterprise policy may exist outside the official Policy Register.**

### 5.10 Policy Review

Formal review at least **annually**. Additional review following: constitutional amendment · material regulatory change · significant governance failure · major acquisition · enterprise restructuring · legal requirement · Board instruction.

*Review does not automatically imply amendment.*

### 5.11 Policy Amendment

**Only the Board may approve amendments to Enterprise Policies.**

Proposed amendments shall include: purpose · business justification · affected policies · constitutional impact · risk assessment · implementation implications · effective date.

Material amendments affecting investor rights require review by the Governance & Ethics Committee before Board consideration.

### 5.12 Governance Assurance

The Audit & Risk Committee shall periodically assess whether: governance structures remain effective · delegated authority is appropriate · policies remain current · conflicts are appropriately managed · records remain complete · Board decisions are properly implemented · governance reporting remains accurate. Findings reported directly to the Board.

|---------------------|-------|-----------------|--------------|---------------------|------------------|-------------------|
| Enterprise Constitution | **A** | C | C | R | C | R |
| Enterprise Policies | **A** | C | C | R | C | R |
| Governance Register | I | I | C | C | C | **A/R** |
| Board Meetings | **A** | I | I | C | R | R |
| Committee Governance | A | R | R | **R** | C | C |
| Conflict Register | I | I | C | **A** | C | R |
| Related-Party Register | I | C | **A/R** | C | C | I |
| Delegation Register | A | I | I | C | **R** | R |

| Annual Governance Review | **A** | C | R | R | C | R |

> **No governance activity shall have more than one Accountable authority.**

### 5.15 Policy Effectiveness

EP-01 is effective only where it demonstrably achieves:

- governance decisions are transparent, documented, and traceable
- fiduciary responsibilities are consistently upheld
- conflicts of interest are disclosed, managed, and independently reviewed
- governance records remain complete, accurate, and auditable
- delegated authority operates within approved limits
- compliance obligations are fulfilled in a timely manner
- investors receive reliable governance oversight and reporting
- the Board maintains effective stewardship over the enterprise, its LLP investment vehicles, and all integrated operating and brand divisions

**The continued confidence of LLP Partners and investors shall be regarded as the ultimate measure of the effectiveness of the Enterprise Governance Policy.**
